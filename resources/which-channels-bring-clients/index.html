The overview

About this guide

The workflow calculates cost per lead, call and client, then gives each channel a verdict: double down, keep, fix or cut. A fictional agency example shows why cheap leads and a busy channel report can tell a different story from signed clients. It also explains what to do with unknown sources, long sales cycles and small samples.

Inside the guide

What's inside

  1. A full-cost channel inventory

    Include ad spend, agency fees, tools and your own hours before comparing channels.

  2. Three AI prompts

    Tag lead sources, calculate the scorecard and compare one month with the next.

  3. The channel scorecard

    Leads, calls, won clients, revenue, cost per client, return and a verdict for each channel.

  4. A worked agency example

    Follow the inputs and the resulting verdicts for a fictional B2B web agency.

  5. Actions and review traps

    What to do with each verdict, plus first-touch attribution, unknown sources, long cycles and small samples.

  • Companies with at least some channel costs and won deals to review.
Put it to work

What you'll be able to do

  • Choose one budget or process change to review next month.
A few questions

Before you start.

What numbers do I need?

The guide uses ninety days of channel costs, leads, first calls, won clients and revenue. You can use a CRM export, spreadsheet or enquiry records. Count your own hours as a cost too.

What if we do not know where clients came from?

The tagging prompt must mark missing sources as Unknown rather than guess. The guide explains how to improve source collection for later runs.

What if our sales cycle is longer than ninety days?

Do not cut a channel just because its open deals have not closed. The guide says to examine the pipeline or use a longer period before judging it.
